## ldx-packer
A GUI to make an [.ldx file](../Specifications/container-format.md) from captures, or to open one, see what it holds and change it. It is a window on [ld-container](ld-container.md), which does all the work (it is run for each step, from the folder of the program), in the style of ld-stacker-gui.

```
ldx-packer [files...]
```

Give it an `.ldx` file to open and `.tbc` or CVBS captures (`.cvbs`, `.cvbsy`) to add, or drop them on the window. A capture is added with its metadata (`.tbc.db` or `.tbc.json`, which must be beside it) and the audio (`.pcm`, or a 16-bit stereo `.wav`, which is read into raw audio first), EFM (`.efm`) and AC3 (`.ac3`) files that have its name. A `.pcm`, `.wav`, `.efm` or `.ac3` file dropped on the window, or chosen with Add capture, goes with the capture that has its name, or else the one picked; audio added to a capture of the file is kept in FLAC when the list is kept as FLAC or Auto. The chroma file of a Y/C pair goes with its luma file. Only one `.ldx` file is opened at a time: the tracks of two files cannot be put in one. The box beside the codec says how the file is written: **File** (with its index) or **Stream** (no index, `ld-container stream`; choosing it asks first, with what a stream cannot do). An opened file starts as it is, and the status line says which it is. Changing it writes the file again (`ld-container index` for the other way). Under the list, **Session** shows the `session.stack` of the file with its sources, and puts a `.stack` file in (Add… or Replace…, with the choice of bringing its `.tbc` sources in) or takes it out (`ld-container stack`, `unstack`). **Metadata…** in the Info panel shows the metadata of the picked capture (how they are kept, the fields they describe, the parameters as JSON), lets the parameters be changed, or replaces all of them with a `.json` or `.db` file (`ld-container metadata`); the tracks that go with the capture are listed with what each holds. The **Shape** box of the Info panel (Not set, 4:3 or 16:9) is the shape video players show the picture in (`ld-container display`): not set is 1:1, the whole image; 4:3 or 16:9 gives the display aspect ratio of the whole image, the same as ld-analyse shows it in (the image is not cut), and sets `isWidescreen` in the metadata with it. **Update** (beside Process) writes the changes in the opened file itself, with no copy, when they are only new metadata, the shape (4:3, 16:9, 1:1), the name of a capture (the **Name** box, now changeable for the captures of the file too) or notes where there were none (`ld-container metadata`, `display`, `rename`, `addtrack --update`); it is greyed when more changed (the order, a track removed or added, the codec, notes that were there, the session, a stream), and Process then writes the file again. Under the list of the tracks that go with a capture, **picking a track** shows what it is and lets its **name** and, for audio, its **sample rate** be changed; the **System** and **Sample rate** of the video are in the Info panel with its Shape (`ld-container settrack`, in the file itself with **Update**, except for FLAC audio whose rate changes, which is coded again by Process). The parameters of the metadata (the video parameters) are in **Metadata…**. In the list, each capture unfolds (the arrow at its left) to show the tracks that go with it, each on a line of its own: the metadata, the audio (pcm), the EFM, the AC3 (RF data, not audio a player plays), and the notes; picking one shows it in the Info panel, where its name and sample rate can be changed. **Parameters…** (next to Metadata… for the video, and in the box of a picked track) shows all the parameters of a track as JSON, to be changed (a value, a key added or taken out): `ld-container settrack`. Any track that goes with a capture can be taken out with its ✕ in the list (or **Remove**), the metadata too after a warning: the file is then written again (`ld-container remove`); the ✕ is ↩ until Process, to give it back. **Add…** (under the list of tracks) opens a dialog to add a file to the capture as a track: the file, what it is (audio, EFM, AC3 or notes, guessed from its name), its name in the file and, for audio, its sample rate (a `.wav` says its own); the file is written again with it (`ld-container addtrack`), since such tracks have data all along the capture. Files dropped on the window are added at once as what their names say. When the **header of an audio track declares another rate than its parameters** (a file written before the header said the real rate: ffplay and VLC play the sound too fast or too slow), the list says "header says 48000 Hz" and the track box offers **Declare 44100 Hz in the header**, which Update does in the file itself. When a shape (4:3 or 16:9) is chosen but the **header of the video track does not say it, or the metadata (`isWidescreen`) say the other**, a warning under Shape says so and a button makes the header say the shape of the metadata (`ld-container display`, in the file itself with Update). **Not set (1:1) is never corrected from the metadata**: it stays 1:1. **A Y/C capture** is one capture in the list (its luma) with its **chroma as a line of its own** under it (marked "chroma"): its name and parameters are changed and it can be taken out alone (the capture is then composite), like any other track. **Add…** has the kind "Chroma of a Y/C capture" to put one on a capture that has none (the luma and the chroma must be both TBC or both CVBS, and have as many fields); the chroma file beside a capture that is added is shown as a line too, and taking it out leaves it out of the file. Taking the capture out, or moving it, takes its chroma with it. **An .ldx file added to the list** (Add capture… or dropped on the window) is not refused any more: its captures come in as lines of their own ("from NAME.ldx", marked new), each with its tracks, and are put in the file when it is written (`ld-container merge`). The tracks of a capture can be taken out before (they are left out), and the capture renamed, its notes changed, or its shape set. The first .ldx file on an empty list is opened as before; the ones after it are merged. The session of the added file is not taken. **The cover of the file** (a picture of the capture, the disc or the tape) is shown at the top of the right panel, whole, in a frame that fits its shape; **Add…**/**Change…** choose a picture (a JPEG, WebP, PNG, GIF or BMP, scaled to at most 800 pixels high, kept as a JPEG when it was a JPEG or a WebP, else as a PNG), **Remove** takes it out and **Undo** gives it back before the file is written (with **Update** it is added at the end of the file, without writing it again). What `ld-container` writes in the history of the file says it was asked by ldx-packer (`via`), and **Help > History** (or File > History) shows it; every step, in the file itself or on a copy, adds its entry (`pack`, `display`, `rename`, `settrack`, `metadata`, `cover`, `merge`...). **Address…** keeps the address of a picture (a web address, or a path relative to the folder of the file, `ld-container cover --source`) in place of the picture, **Link…** sets the web address that opens in the browser when the cover is clicked (`--link`, empty takes it out); the cover is shown with the same widget as the other tools (a web address is fetched only by its **Show the picture** button), **Undo** undoes the picture, the address and the link together, and **Update** writes them at the end of the file, without writing it again.

### Window

- **Captures** (left): one row for each capture, as the list of ld-stacker-gui: a bar and a tint of the colour of the capture, its name, what it is (system, number of fields or frames, duration, codec) and a tag that says how it stands: *kept*, *edited* (files or notes changed), *recode* (its samples will be kept in another codec), *new* or *removed*. The ✕ button takes a capture out (↩ keeps it again); the arrows put the captures in another order: the file is then written again with its tracks numbered to follow the list (`ld-container reorder`), and what names a track follows the new numbers, the session of the file included. **Create LDX…** (**Process…** when a file is open) asks where to write; **Open LDX…** loads a file.
- **Codec**: how the samples are kept: *Auto* (FLAC for the TBC and the audio, FFV1 for CVBS), *FLAC*, *FFV1* (CVBS only) or *Raw*. *As they are* is for an opened file, and leaves the tracks in the codec they have.
- **Info** (right, key I): what is known of the capture that is picked (kind, system, sample rate, size of a field, number of fields, duration, codec, track number, the track it maps if it is a mapping, its chroma track), the **tracks that go with it** (metadata, audio, EFM, AC3), which can be added (**Add…**, a `.pcm`, `.efm` or `.ac3` file) and taken out (**Remove**), and its **notes**, kept in the file as a track of annotations. The name of a capture that is to be added can be changed there; the one of a capture that is in the file cannot.
- **Check** reads every block of the file in the order of the file and by its place in its track (`ld-container verify`).

### Writing

**Create LDX…** or **Process…** asks for a file (a copy, or over the file that was opened) and whether to check it once written. The list is written as a series of `ld-container` commands (`pack`, `add`, `remove`, `recompress`, `addtrack`, `verify`), each making a file in a hidden folder beside the output; the last one is renamed to the file asked for, and nothing is left if the work is stopped or fails. A file that is written over its original is replaced only when everything has been done.

### Menu

- **File**: *Create LDX from a new list* (Ctrl+N), *Open LDX…* (Ctrl+O), *Add capture…* (Ctrl+T), *Unpack all the tracks…* (`ld-container unpack`), *Check the file*, *Show the history…* (what was done to the file, by which tool and when), *Quit*.
- **View**: the Info panel, and the colour themes and widget styles of the other windows (kept between sessions); `--force-dark-theme` forces the dark theme for one session.

`--screenshot file.png` writes what the window shows and quits, and `--size WxH` sets its size.

**What a person says of a capture.** The panel of information has **Medium**, **Reference**, **Disc copy**, **Capture no.** and **Face** under the sample rate (each is "not said" to begin with, and "not said" takes the value out of the file): they are what the `.rfx` archives of RF-tools say of a capture, so that they can be copied as they are (`ld-container tag`). They are parameters of the video track, changed in the file itself by **Update** like the system and the sample rate. **System** has PAL, NTSC, PAL-M, **SECAM** (the 625 lines of PAL) and **NTSC-VHD** (the 525 lines of NTSC, a VideoDisc): the track and the metadata of the capture say the same (`ld-container settrack`); no decoder makes colour of SECAM. A track `ldx.rfsource` that says where the capture comes from (the RF file it was decoded from) is listed with the tracks of the capture, and is not taken for its metadata.

**Editing them for every capture, and where it comes from.** Medium, Reference, Disc copy, Capture no. and Face are editable for **every capture of the list**: for one that is in the file they are changed with **Update** (`ld-container tag`, `settrack`); for one that is added from a `.tbc` they are given to `ld-container pack` or `add` (`--medium`, `--reference`, `--copy`, `--capture-number`, `--face`), and for one taken from another `.ldx` file, a `tag` step after the merge. **Where from…** (beside Metadata…) opens the information of the track `ldx.rfsource`: the RF file the capture was decoded from (its name, size, SHA-256, sample format, bit depth, RF sample rate, date, the compressed file it was made from, the archive and the signal), with the files that were beside it, which can be taken out. **Replace with a .json file…** takes the entry of a capture of an `.rfx` archive (`rfx info`), **Clear** takes everything away, and the members the window does not show are kept. It is written with Update, in the file itself (`ld-container rfsource`).
